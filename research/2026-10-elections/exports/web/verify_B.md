## What matters for exports (10 lines)
1. China is the binding constraint on beef. Brazil hit its 1.1 Mt safeguard quota and has paid 67% on Chinese beef since 1 Oct 2026. ABIEC expects total beef exports to fall about 10% in 2026, and 2025 China beef sales were $8.9bn of $18.3bn. Lula's government failed to get quota flexibility. Flávio says he would "negotiate". The safeguard is global and runs to end-2028, so it is not partisan coercion.
2. Soy is the largest exposure, at more than one-third of ~$100bn exports to China. The US–China truce is already shifting share back to the US: China's share of Brazil's soy exports fell from 75% to 69% in H1. Brazil diverted the volume and its total soy exports still grew 8%.
3. Precedent says partisan rhetoric has not cost Brazil trade with China. Bolsonaro 2019–22 and the Eduardo spats produced no Chinese trade measures, and exports to China hit a record $89bn in 2022. Sanitary suspension lengths reflect protocol and politics: about 3 months in 2021 vs about 1 month in 2023.
4. Coercion analogues set the tail risk at roughly A$20bn/yr for Australia over about 4.5 years, a 70% fall for Canadian canola over about 3 years, and an ~80–90% fall for Lithuania. Diversion was easy for fungible commodities (coal, cotton) and hard for niche ones (lobster, wine). China depends more on Brazilian soy and iron ore than it did on any of those.
5. Taiwan, Huawei or Pix/UnionPay moves under Flávio are the plausible triggers. I found no Flávio statement on Taiwan or BYD.
6. The Middle East (~$3.1bn of chicken, ~30% of chicken exports) is exposed to Flávio's pledge to move the embassy to Jerusalem within 6 months of a 2027 term. The 2019 precedent was an informal plant delisting, with no measurable sustained loss found.
7. Israel trade is small: $0.73bn of Brazilian exports in 2024 and ~$0.57bn in 2025. A Flávio realignment adds little upside.
8. India is a growth channel under Lula: a $30bn-by-2030 target and Mercosur–India PTA expansion talks launched 14 Sep 2026. Mercosur–UAE CEPA is advanced but unsigned.
9. Sanitary access is institutional and continuous. MAPA counts 639 market openings since 2023. China approved 17 beef plants under Bolsonaro in 2019 and 24 under Lula in 2024. Tereza Cristina, Bolsonaro's minister, is advising Flávio. HPAI bans lasted about 4 months in the EU and about 6 months in China.
10. Critical minerals: Law 15.506 (Sep 2026) gives the presidential council Cimce a veto over changes of corporate control. That puts the USA Rare Earth–Serra Verde deal ($2.8bn, US-backed offtake) and any US federal agreement within presidential discretion. Lula's camp is sovereignty-first, and the US has signed only with Goiás state. Flávio would likely fast-track. China's Oct-2025 rare-earth control suspension expires 10 Nov 2026 unless renewed.
