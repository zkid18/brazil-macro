## 10-line summary: what matters for exports
1. The EU–Mercosur iTA is already applied (since 1 May 2026), so a Flávio win does not undo it. EU-side risk comes from the CJEU opinion (~2027) and the 2027 elections in France, Italy and Poland, not from Brasília.
2. Early gains are visible: Brazil's exports to the EU rose +23.5% y/y in May–Aug 2026 to $20.4bn, against $49.8bn for the full 2025 year (C15, C18).
3. A bigger near-term hit than any tariff: since 3 Sep 2026 the EU has suspended Brazilian beef, poultry, eggs and honey imports over antimicrobials. That puts ~$1.8bn/yr of beef at risk, and only poultry and honey are close to relisting (C19, C20).
4. The ag safeguard regulation (2026/687, with a >5% price trigger) and the TRQs cap upside on beef, poultry, sugar and ethanol. A deforestation rebound under a weaker environmental policy would give France and Poland political cover to trigger safeguards.
5. The EUDR applies from 30 Dec 2026 and Brazil is "standard risk". About half of Brazil's soymeal (11.7 Mt) plus coffee (~14% of EU exports) and beef are exposed. A rise in deforestation could prompt reclassification to high risk.
6. The environmental baseline is strong under Lula (PRODES −11%, DETER −37%), while Flávio's programme omits Paris and the NDC and promises to cut "amarras ambientais". This is the main channel through which the election changes EU market access.
7. CBAM exposure is modest: ~$1.7bn of iron and steel to the EU, and low-carbon pig iron may even gain. EU ETS is around €84/t. The SBCE will not price carbon before ~2030.
8. Argentina is an export market in decline: −19% in H1 2026 (autos −28%) on Chinese competition. A Flávio–Milei alignment could loosen the CET and Mercosur rules (US deals), and diplomatic ties were downgraded in Aug 2026 under Lula.
9. In oil, crude was the top export in 2025 ($44.7bn, ~45% to China), Brent spiked in 2026, and Foz do Amazonas has made discoveries. Both camps keep Petrobras state-owned (C57). The difference lies in pricing and divestment, not ownership.
10. In FX, the market read is pro-Flávio: BRL to ~5.00 (−4%) and Ibovespa +7.8% on 5 Oct. A stronger BRL under a fiscal-adjustment scenario (JPM +6%) would weigh on exporters' BRL margins. The Selic is 13.75%.
