## 3. The 2025 tariff as a natural experiment (empirical elasticities)

**3.1 Design.** Treatment = `exports_to_us` from 2025-08; controls = `exports_to_china`, `exports_to_eu`, rest-of-world (= total − US − China − EU), total. Three estimators, all reported with CIs:
1. **Same-month YoY DiD:** YoY(post months) − YoY(pre months Jan–Jul 2025), for post windows of 4 months (Aug–Nov 2025), 5 months (Aug–Dec, before the Nov relief fully lands), 13 months (Aug 2025–Aug 2026). US −19.3 pts on the 13-m window is the anchor.
2. **Seasonal-factor counterfactual:** seasonal factor s_m = mean(2015–2024) of month m share of annual total (0.3 shows the profile); counterfactual US_t = s_m × trailing pre-tariff annual level × (1 + pre-trend 4.8%); gap = Σ(actual − counterfactual) → $bn/yr and % of baseline. Placebo: apply the same estimator with the break at each month 2016-01…2024-12 and report where −19 pts sits in the placebo distribution (p-value by placebo rank). Same for China (expect the 2018 and 2020 soy episodes to produce comparable placebo moves — say so).
3. **Two-year change** (post 13 m vs the same months two years earlier) to strip the H2-2024 China base effect: US −11.4%, China +4.1%, EU +12.7%, total +7.7%.

**3.2 Diversion accounting.** US gap G_US ≈ −$8.5bn (13 m). Non-US gain above own pre-trend: China +$26.2bn, EU +$3.3bn, rest ≈ +$10bn — far larger than G_US, so diversion cannot be identified by residual; it is confounded by (i) the US–China soy substitution (soy +30.8% post vs −7.7% pre), (ii) the 2026 Brent shock (oil tonnes +27.8%), (iii) beef/coffee price booms. Report a **bounded diversion rate**: lower bound 0 (all non-US growth unrelated), upper bound min(1, non-US gain / |G_US|) = 1, central = share of the US product mix that is fungible commodity (external fact: composition of Brazil→US exports — crude, semi-finished steel, aircraft, coffee, beef, pulp, OJ; sourced in 5.1). Net national loss = G_US × (1 − diversion rate) → range −$8.5bn (no diversion) to ≈ −$1bn (near-full diversion at discounted prices). Price cost of diversion: compare `oil_export_unit_value` relative to `brent_usd` (US$/t ÷ US$/bbl × 7.33) pre vs post; and `pulp_unit_value` — if realised prices relative to benchmark fell post-tariff, that is the ToT-type cost.

**3.3 Thresholds (pre-register in `report.md` before results):** tariff effect "material" if the 13-m DiD < −10 pts with placebo p < 0.10; "diversion dominant" if total exports' DiD ≥ 0 and two-year total change ≥ +5%; "China base effect dominant" if two-year China change < half the one-year change. Expected read-out from 0.3: material US effect, diversion dominant, China base effect dominant — i.e. L4's framing ("pivot to China") overstates the policy content.

**3.4 Elasticity outputs for the scenario matrix** (`tariff_episode.csv`): E_tariff = realised % fall in US-bound exports per tariff event (−14 to −25% for a 50% tariff with ~40–50% of lines exempt — exemption share is an external fact); G_US $bn/yr; diversion bounds; time profile (months to trough; whether Jun–Aug 2026 US YoY of +2.3/−6.4/+12.1 marks recovery after the Nov 2025 relief — test with a 3-m mean vs the Aug–Nov 2025 trough).

## 4. Methods for mapping exports → GDP, plus analogues

**4.1 Two mappings, always both, bounds not points.**
- **Direct demand channel** (for volume/market-access shocks: tariffs, sanitary bans, quotas): ΔGDP% ≈ (ΔX / GDP_usd) × (1 − m) × k, with import content m ∈ [0.10, 0.20] (commodities low) and multiplier k ∈ [1.0, 1.5]; GDP = `wb/NY.GDP.MKTP.CD.BR` 2025 ($2,280bn). Worked: ΔX = −$8bn → −0.35% × [0.8–0.9] × [1.0–1.5] = **−0.28% to −0.47% of GDP level**, one-off.
- **ToT channel** (for price-type shocks: commodity states, realised-price discounts from diversion): Δ(GDP pc growth, pts) = β × 100·Δlog ToT, β ∈ [0.25, 0.29] with the bootstrap CI (recompute: annual `wb/NY.GDP.PCAP.KD.ZG.BR` on Δlog annual-mean `wb/TOT.BRA`, 1992–2025, block bootstrap 3, 2,000 draws; also with `wb/TT.PRI.MRCH.XD.WD.BR` 2006–2024). State plainly that β is reduced-form and co-moves with the global cycle, so it is an upper bound for a pure price shock; and that ToT is a ratio, so an oil-price rise lifts both exports (12.8%) and imports (fuel, fertilizer) — net via `trade_balance`.
- Convert any export $ shock into ToT terms only if it is a price shock: Δlog ToT ≈ (price-type ΔX / X) × (share of exports affected). Volume shocks do not enter the ToT mapping.

**4.2 FX competitiveness.** Re-estimate Δlog export volume (`wb/TX.QTY.MRCH.XD.WD.BR`) on Δlog REER (`wb/PX.REX.REER.BR`) and Δlog ToT, lags 0–1, 2006–2024, bootstrap CI (expect ≈ 0, 0.8). Monthly: Δlog `exports_total` (12-m sum) on Δlog `brl_usd` (12-m mean) lags 0–12, 2015–2026. Read-out for the channels list: a weaker BRL under either candidate raises exporters' BRL margins (`total_return_brl@SUZB`, H6 shows the equity side) and planted area with a lag, but does not move USD export values in-sample; FX matters mainly for imports and the current account (`wb/BN.CAB.XOKA.GD.ZS.BR`), and for the fertilizer bill (`fertilizer_imports`, `fertilizer_dependency_index`).

**4.3 Historical analogues (descriptive, each with its confounder named):**
- Bolsonaro 2019–22 vs Lula III 2023–26 destination mix (0.7): lean did not change the mix. Confounders: COVID, 2021–22 commodity boom (ToT +13.4% in 2021).
- Bolsonaro 2019–22 EU access: EU exports rose 36.3 $bn/yr annualised despite the Amazon-fires diplomatic crisis (2019) and the EU–Mercosur freeze — but EU share fell 14.5 → 13.9, and the agreement stalled for the whole term (external fact). Under Lula III EU share 14.2 and the agreement concluded (external fact). - 2025 tariff: template for "US coercion = −$8bn/yr with heavy diversion".
