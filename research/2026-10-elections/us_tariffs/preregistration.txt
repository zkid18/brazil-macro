PRE-REGISTRATION: US tariffs on Brazil under Lula III
Written 2026-10-06, after the section 0 anchors reproduced (results.json "anchors.*", mismatches = []) and after the
single pull us_ncm_m (Brazil->US by NCM-8, monthly, 2023-01..2026-09, 121,957 rows). No other pull, no estimate
and no tariff-list mapping had been run when this file was written.

CONVENTIONS
- Seed 0. OLS by np.linalg.lstsq. Cluster bootstrap by HS4 (2,000 draws) for product panels and by UF for state
  panels. Placebo by permuting treatment status across HS4 (2,000). Event-study placebo: 2,000 dates >= 90 days
  from any event; estimation window [-250,-121].
- CIs reported: 80% and 90%. Headline window for markets: [-5,+5].
- Verdict rule (every hypothesis): "supports Hx" when the baseline sign matches, >= 80% of robustness cells share
  that sign, and the 80% cluster-bootstrap CI excludes zero. "Consistent with Hx, not proof" when the CI straddles
  zero but the sign and the robustness majority match. Otherwise "does not support Hx". The competing H' gets the
  same rule. Both can fail (null result); that is reported as such.
- Regimes: R0 <= 2025-03-11; R1 2025-03-12..2025-08-05; R2 2025-08-06..2025-11-12; R3 2025-11-13..2026-02-23;
  R4 2026-02-24..2026-07-21; R5 >= 2026-07-22. Statuses per NCM: covered (full added rate), exempt (Annex),
  232 (sectoral). Main control group excludes crude 2709 (exempt but fell for non-tariff reasons).
- Gross loss by HS4 = 2024 value x (1 - exp(coef)); net = gross x (1 - d).

HYPOTHESES (symmetric pairs)

H1 (small and diffuse): net national loss after diversion <= 0.2% of GDP ($4.6bn/yr) AND no single HS4 lost more
   than $1bn/yr.
H1' (concentrated and large): lines at the full rate lost >= 25% of their 2024 value, the top 10 treated HS4 carry
   >= 70% of the gross loss, and the top 5 states carry >= 75%.
   Statistic: triple-diff gross loss by HS4 (5.1), diversion-adjusted (5.2).

H2 (commodities divert, manufactures do not): diversion rate d >= 0.6 for the commodity group (0901, 0202, 1602,
   7201, 7207, 7224, 4703, 1701, 1502, 2401, 2818; crude 2709 as a special case) and d <= 0.3 for the manufactured
   group (8802, 8807, 8429, 8504, 6403, 6402, 9403, 4409, 4418, 4412, 4407, 4011, 6802, 9306, 7304).
H2': no difference, |d_comm - d_manuf| < 0.2.
   Statistic: d_i = clip(dROW_i / |dUS_i|, 0, 1) from the treated-vs-exempt DiD on exports to non-US destinations;
   cluster bootstrap over HS4 for the group means.

H3 (the US bears the cost on low-substitutability lines): for lines where Brazil's share of US imports > 30%,
   (i) Brazil's US unit value relative to its ROW unit value did not fall by more than 5% in R2;
   (ii) US landed unit values from Brazil rose relative to competitors;
   (iii) the Nov-2025 relief is concentrated on those lines (relieved-value share among >30%-share lines vs
   <10%-share lines).
H3' (Brazil absorbed): relative unit values fell >= 5% and the relief list is uncorrelated with the Brazil share.
   Statistic: unit-value DiD (5.3), Comtrade mirror (5.4), 2x2 revealed-preference table.

H4 (trade-only escalation costs < 1% of GDP; only financial sanctions cross it): in the matrix, rung (c)'s central
   net loss < $23bn (1% of 2025 GDP) and rung (d) exceeds $23bn only when the financial block is included.
H4': trade effects alone in rung (c) exceed 1% of GDP because manufactured lines do not divert.
   Modelling hypothesis: verdict = the computed matrix under the central elasticity set; low/high reported.

H5 (markets treated the tariff as noise): mean CAR of escalation dates on brl_usd, ibovespa_usd,
   gov_real_yield_10y, Embraer and the exposed-firm basket is inside the placebo 5-95% band, [-5,+5].
H5': escalation dates have |CAR| beyond the 90th placebo percentile and de-escalation dates the mirror sign, with an
   exact permutation p < 0.10 for the escalation-vs-de-escalation group difference.

H6 (state concentration without an employment signal): five states carry >= 75% of the treated-line loss, and
   exposed states' PIM-PF and unemployment do not diverge from unexposed states by more than one cross-state sd
   after Aug 2025.
H6': divergence > 1 sd in the direction of the exposure.

H7 (the 2025 fall was mostly not the 40% tariff): crude (exempt) plus Section 232 steel explain >= 50% of the
   Aug-2025..Jul-2026 fall in US-bound exports (year-on-year, same months).
H7': lines at the 40% explain >= 50%.
   Statistic: decomposition of the y/y gap by tariff status.

H8 (revealed preference on the US side): the Nov-2025 relief covered the lines with the highest US CPI salience x
   Brazil share; relief is predicted by US consumer prices, not by Brazilian concessions.
H8': relief tracked the Lula-Trump meetings (6 Oct and 26 Oct 2025) and covered lines regardless of CPI salience.
   Statistic: 2x2 of relieved vs not x (CPI salience high vs low), plus the dates. Note in advance: the date test
   cannot separate the two (both the meetings and the CPI prints precede 20 Nov); the cross-section is the test.

ROBUSTNESS GRID (5.1)
HS4 vs NCM-8; log levels vs share of world; with/without crude 2709; with/without 232 lines; pre-period
Jan 2023-Jun 2025 vs Jan 2024-Jun 2025; drop coffee and beef. 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 x 2 = up to 64 cells (some collapse).

COVERAGE STOP RULE (computed from the HS6-mapped lists x 2024 US-bound exports)
R5: full 301+forced-labour 16.5% +/- 3 pts; exempt 52.7% +/- 5; Section 232 24.2% +/- 5 (MDIC, A23).
R2: full 50% 35.9% +/- 4; 232 19.5% +/- 4 (A04).
R3 (after Nov relief): 22% +/- 4 at 40% (A10).
R4 (post-SCOTUS): 46% +/- 5 at no extra tariff (A18).
A miss beyond tolerance means the mapping is wrong: inspect and fix; if it cannot be fixed, document and continue.

WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE READ
- If the placebo distribution of the treated-vs-exempt coefficient is wide (rank p > 0.10), the loss estimates are
  reported as "consistent with", and the escalation matrix uses analogue-bounded elasticities instead.
- Market CARs from a single country-specific event series are underpowered; null results are the expected default.
